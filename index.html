Virtual Patient Clinic

An interactive patient simulator for medical students. Students interview a realistic AI patient, by voice, then they can order examinations and tests, and present their thinking and receive feedback based on what they said and did.

Practicing with standardized patients is one of the best ways to learn a clinical interview, and one of the hardest to schedule. Virtual Patient gives students a patient they can see any time: they talk to her, she answers in her own voice with her face moving as she speaks, and she reveals what she knows only when asked well, sometimes only after the student builds rapport.

After the encounter, students give a summary, differential and plan, and the app scores the history, communication and reasoning, with every point linked to the moment in the transcript where it happened. Faculty write the cases, assign them, and review, adjust and release the scores.

What's in it

  • Voice interviews with patients who sigh, worry and react to how the student talks: rushed questions and interruptions make them guarded.
  • Patients you can see: photoreal synthetic faces lip-synced on BYU's own GPUs, or animated 3D patients.
  • Exam and orders from one fixed menu, with the case's findings, lab values and images.
  • Evidence-linked feedback on history, communication (Calgary-Cambridge) and clinical reasoning, which faculty can review and override.
  • Case authoring with AI drafting, and dials for each patient's talkativeness, openness, assertiveness, emotion and patience.

Where it stands

Version 0.2.0 (October 2026), a pilot on BYU's servers, reachable on the BYU VPN or campus network.

Next: getting ready for a first student cohort (network access, clinical review of the cases, phone support), then instructor live observation, presentations to an attending, and test mode. The sample cases and faces are drafts awaiting School of Medicine review.

Virtual Patient is a BYU Computer Science project with the BYU School of Medicine, led by Quinn Snell. No real patient data is used anywhere: every case and face is invented.
